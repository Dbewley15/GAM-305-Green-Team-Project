Game Concept and Brainstorming

Theme/Concept: Space Cats on an Alien Planet

The Green Team will develop a first-person exploration and combat game set on a mysterious alien planet. The player takes on the role of a space cat exploring the ancient ruins of a long-lost alien civilization. The planet contains abandoned structures, strange technology, dangerous areas, and hostile alien creatures.

The player will explore the ruins, navigate the environment, encounter enemies, collect pickups, and complete objectives throughout the level. Elevators and jump pads will help the player reach different areas of the alien ruins, while the enemy tracking capability will help the player locate or monitor hostile enemies.

The visual style will combine a futuristic space setting with ancient alien ruins. The environment may include abandoned temples, damaged technology, strange alien structures, glowing objects, rocky terrain, and other environmental details that help create the feeling of exploring an unknown planet.

The team will collaborate on the final setting, visual style, space-cat character design, enemy designs, objectives, level layout, and implementation of the four selected gameplay elements. The goal is to create a playable first-person experience that demonstrates the required scenario and additional elements while remaining achievable within the course timeline.

Alpha Stage

The goal for the Alpha submission is to have the core gameplay and major systems implemented in a playable state. 
The first-person level should have its basic alien planet and ruins layout, player movement, enemies, pickups, elevators, jump pads, and initial enemy tracking functionality.

Basic UI elements and gameplay objectives should also be in place. The team will use the Alpha stage to identify bugs, missing features, and systems that need additional development.

Progress will be tracked through the team's task list, Discord updates, testing, and repository commits.

Beta Stage

The goal for the Beta submission is to have all major gameplay elements implemented and functioning together in a mostly complete level. The team will focus on polishing the alien planet environment, ancient ruins, UI, gameplay mechanics, enemy behavior, pickups, elevators, jump pads, and enemy tracking system.

During Beta, the team will concentrate on testing, bug fixing, balancing, and improving the overall player experience. Progress will be tracked through completed task items, testing results, Discord updates, and repository commits.

Final Development\
Complete remaining gameplay features.\
Finish the alien planet and ancient ruins environment.\
Fix remaining bugs and technical issues.\
Polish the environment, space-cat theme, and UI.\
Conduct final playtesting.\
Make necessary adjustments based on testing.\
Verify that all required scenario and additional elements are functional.\
Prepare the final project for submission.\

Team Communication

Primary Communication Method: Discord

Meeting/Communication Frequency: Sunday through Tuesday

The team will use the Green Team Discord thread and voice channel for project communication, planning, questions, and development coordination. Team members are expected to check Discord regularly during the scheduled communication days and provide updates when completing assigned tasks or encountering problems.

Discord will also be used to discuss problems, share progress, coordinate development tasks, and make decisions that affect the project.

Task Assignment and Progress Reporting

The team will use a shared task list to assign and track individual responsibilities. Each task will include the assigned team member, a description of the task, and its current status.

Tasks will be organized into the following categories:

Not Started\
In Progress\
Testing\
Completed\

Team members will report their progress through Discord and update the task list as work is completed. If a team member encounters a problem or is unable to complete a task, they will communicate the issue to the team so responsibilities or deadlines can be adjusted as needed.

Repository commits will also be used to track development progress and provide a record of completed work.

Alpha and Beta Progress Tracking

The team will use the task list, Discord communication, repository commits, and playtesting to monitor project progress.

For the Alpha stage, progress will be measured by whether the core gameplay systems and required gameplay elements are implemented and playable.

For the Beta stage, progress will be measured by whether all major systems are working together, the level is mostly complete, and the project has moved into testing, bug fixing, and polish.

This process will allow the team to identify unfinished work early and make adjustments before the final submission.

Module Three Team Quality Assurance and Testing Plan
Quality Assurance Communication and Collaboration

The Green Team will use Discord as the primary means of communication for quality assurance and testing. Team members will use the team's Discord thread and voice channel to discuss test results, report bugs, review changes, and determine which issues need to be addressed.

The team will collaboratively develop and maintain the test plan in the GitHub repository. Team members will contribute test cases based on their areas of responsibility and review testing results with the rest of the team. The shared test plan will be updated whenever changes are made to the game or design document.

The team will use the shared task list, Discord, and GitHub repository to communicate testing responsibilities and track progress.

Testing Schedule

Testing will take place throughout the development cycle instead of waiting until the end of the project.

Play Test - Preproduction Stage

During preproduction and early development, the team will conduct play tests of individual mechanics and early level builds.

The purpose of the Play Test stage is to determine whether the basic gameplay systems are working as intended and whether the level design supports the planned player experience.

The team will test:

Player movement and controls\
Basic level navigation\
Player interaction with the environment\
Enemy behavior\
Pickups\
Elevators\
Jump pads\
Enemy tracking\
Basic UI\
Level objectives\
Alien planet and ruins navigation\

Issues discovered during Play Testing will be reported and assigned before the affected feature moves into the next development stage.

Demo - Before Marketing Demo

Before the project is presented as a demonstration, the team will conduct a full game playthrough using the current build.

The Demo test will verify that the major gameplay systems work together and that a player can complete the intended level without encountering major problems.

The team will check:

Complete level progression\
Player movement and controls\
Enemy encounters\
Pickup functionality\
Elevator functionality\
Jump pad functionality\
Enemy tracking functionality\
UI and objectives\
Level transitions\
Collision and physics\
Audio and visual elements\
Alien ruins and environment\
Game performance\
Major bugs and gameplay blockers\

Any critical problems discovered during the Demo test will be prioritized before the project is demonstrated.

Code Release - Test Plan Verification

Before a code release or final build is submitted, the team will perform a final test using the current version of the test plan.

The team will verify that previously reported bugs have been fixed and that fixes have not created new problems in other parts of the game.

The Code Release test will include:

Running all required test cases.\
Confirming that required gameplay elements are functional.\
Rechecking previously failed test cases.\
Testing recently changed features.\
Checking for new bugs caused by changes.\
Confirming that major gameplay systems work together.\
Confirming that the current build matches the design requirements.\
Recording final Pass/Fail results.\

A code release will not be considered ready until critical failures have been addressed or documented for the team.

Updating the Test Plan

The test plan will be updated whenever changes are made to the game, gameplay mechanics, level design, assets, UI, or design document.

When a new feature is added, the team will determine whether a new test case is needed. If an existing feature is changed, the related test case will be reviewed and updated to reflect the new expected behavior.

The team will review the test plan during major development milestones, including Alpha, Beta, Demo, and Code Release. This will help make sure that the testing requirements remain consistent with the current version of the game.

Changes to the test plan will be communicated through Discord and recorded in the GitHub repository so the team can see what was changed and why.

Bug Reporting

Bugs will be reported through the team's shared task list and discussed through Discord.

Each bug report should include:

Bug title
Date reported\
Team member who reported the bug\
Description of the problem\
Steps to reproduce the problem\
Expected result\
Actual result\
Severity or priority\
Game/build version\
Assigned team member\
Current status\

Screenshots or video clips may also be included when they help explain the problem.

Critical bugs that prevent the player from progressing or prevent a major game system from functioning will be communicated to the team immediately.

Bug Tracking and Changes Over Time

The team will track bugs through the shared task list and GitHub repository. Each reported bug will receive a status so the team can follow it from discovery through resolution.

Bug statuses will include:

Reported - The problem has been identified but has not been reviewed.\
Confirmed - The team has verified that the problem exists.\
In Progress - A team member is working on a solution.\
Testing - A potential fix has been completed and needs to be tested.\
Resolved - The fix has been tested and the original problem no longer occurs.\
Closed - The team has confirmed that the issue is resolved.\
Deferred - The team has decided that the issue will be addressed later because of its priority or available development time.\

When a bug is fixed, the team member responsible for the fix will update the task and provide information about the change. The team will then retest the affected feature and any related features that could have been affected by the change.

GitHub commits will provide an additional record of development changes. The team will use these records to help identify when changes were made and determine whether a new problem may have been introduced by a recent update.

This process will allow the team to maintain a clear record of bugs, fixes, testing results, and changes throughout the development cycle.

## Module Four Project Log - Team Reflection

### QA and Testing Process: What Went Well?

The team’s QA and testing process was successful because testing occurred throughout Alpha development rather than only after the project was completed. Team members tested individual mechanics as they were implemented and also tested updated versions of the project after changes were merged through GitHub. Discord was used to communicate test results, errors, and possible solutions between team members.

Testing by multiple team members was especially helpful when identifying integration problems. After graphics changes were merged, Donovan and Colby both encountered problems opening the updated project. This confirmed that the problem affected the shared project rather than only one team member’s installation. The team was then able to investigate the First Person Character Blueprint and Tripo3DUEBridge plugin issues.

Gameplay mechanics were also tested throughout development. The team tested player movement and shooting, projectile damage, enemy health and destruction, enemy roaming, level navigation, collision, elevators, jump pads, pickups, and UI functionality. Pull requests and approvals provided another opportunity for team members to review changes before they were incorporated into the shared project.

### Bugs: Identifying and Correcting

Several bugs were identified and corrected during Alpha development. An earlier projectile collision issue caused the player to collide with projectiles when moving forward while shooting. Colby corrected the issue by adjusting the projectile spawn location.

A larger integration problem occurred after graphics and character assets were added to the project. The updated project produced errors involving BP_FirstPersonCharacter and the Tripo3DUEBridge plugin. The First Person Character Blueprint issue was corrected. The Tripo3D plugin continued to create compatibility problems on other team members’ systems, so the team temporarily disabled the plugin in the .uproject file to allow Alpha development and testing to continue. The plugin compatibility issue will continue to be investigated as the project moves into Beta.

Additional gameplay bugs were found through testing. Ben identified a collision problem with doors in the level and corrected the collision so they functioned properly.

The elevator initially had a collision problem that prevented the player from standing on the moving platform. Adjusting the platform’s collision settings corrected the issue and allowed the player to ride the elevator as it moved between levels.

Enemy roaming also revealed a navigation problem during testing. Although the enemy appeared to be standing inside the Nav Mesh, the enemy mesh was affecting navigation and creating an unusable area beneath the enemy. Disabling the mesh’s ability to affect navigation corrected the problem. The enemies were then able to select random reachable locations and roam around the level correctly.

### QA and Testing: What Would We Do Differently?

For future development, the team would test major changes on more than one team member’s computer before merging them into the main branch. This is especially important when introducing plugins, imported assets, or other external dependencies. The Tripo3D issue demonstrated that a feature working correctly on one team member’s installation does not guarantee that it will function correctly for the rest of the team.

The team would also perform combined-build testing more frequently. Individual feature testing was useful for identifying problems with specific mechanics, but testing the complete build more often would allow integration problems to be discovered earlier. This would provide more time to correct issues before a milestone deadline.

### Tools or Techniques: Helpful

Git and GitHub were successful tools for Alpha development. Branches allowed team members to work independently, while pull requests and approvals allowed changes to be reviewed before being merged into the shared project. When project-loading problems occurred, a backup branch was also created to preserve a known working version of the project.

Discord was successful for team communication and troubleshooting. Team members used Discord to coordinate development, report bugs, share screenshots and error messages, request pull request approvals, arrange voice calls, and communicate progress.

Unreal Engine Blueprints were useful for rapidly implementing and testing gameplay mechanics. Blueprints were used for systems including projectile behavior, enemy health and damage, enemy roaming, the elevator, pickups, and UI functionality. Unreal Engine’s Nav Mesh system was also useful for implementing roaming enemy behavior while allowing the existing level geometry to determine where enemies could travel.

### Tools or Techniques: Not Helpful

The Tripo3DUEBridge plugin was not successful as a shared development tool during the Alpha stage. Although it functioned on the system where the graphics work was performed, other team members could not reliably open the project while the plugin was enabled. Attempts to rebuild the plugin were unsuccessful, so it was temporarily disabled to allow Alpha development to continue.

This experience showed that introducing external project dependencies before verifying compatibility across the team can create integration problems. During Beta development, plugins and similar external dependencies should be tested on a separate branch and verified by multiple team members before being incorporated into the shared project.

### Team Approach: Decision to Use Tools and Techniques

The team’s initial project analysis divided development responsibilities according to each member’s assigned role while allowing responsibilities to be shared when necessary. GitHub branches and pull requests supported this approach because programmers, level designers, artists, and UI developers could work independently and then combine their work through a controlled review process.

Discord supported the team’s planned communication approach by allowing members to coordinate work remotely and quickly discuss problems. Direct voice communication was also used when Donovan and Ben worked together on the level layout.

The decision to use Unreal Engine Blueprints supported the team’s goal of producing a functional Alpha prototype within the available development time. The team prioritized functional gameplay systems before final visual polish, allowing the required mechanics to be implemented and tested while other areas of the project continued to develop.

### Alpha Stage Status Assessment

The team successfully completed the Alpha stage and produced a functional, playable Alpha build. The final Alpha contains player movement and shooting, projectile damage, multiple enemies, enemy health and destruction, enemy roaming, an elevator, jump pad functionality, Health, Armor, and Speed pickups, UI functionality, a death screen, and a victory screen.

The Alpha testing process demonstrated that the major gameplay systems can function together within the playable level. Issues discovered during development were tested and corrected as the team integrated its individual contributions into the shared project.

For Beta development, the team will build upon the completed Alpha by refining the level, improving graphics and environmental presentation, expanding and polishing gameplay where appropriate, and continuing combined-build testing. The team will also continue investigating the Tripo3D plugin compatibility issue or use an alternative workflow for future visual assets.

Major changes and external dependencies will be tested across multiple team members’ systems before merging whenever possible. The team will continue using Discord, GitHub, pull requests, and playtesting to track progress and identify problems. These adjustments will help reduce integration issues and keep development on schedule for the Beta deadline.
